ESD Analysis

Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) is defined as the transient electric current between two objects at different electrostatic potentials causing an ESD event. Due to shrinking geometries of nanometer designs, devices are at a higher risk to ESD exposure and are prone to damage.

To prevent an ESD event, you can place ESD devices at power/ground pads to provide an efficient discharge path between one bump to another.

Calculation of effective resistance between a large pair of nodes is a difficult engineering problem with high computational complexity. Although there are graph-based search algorithms, they only identify the least resistance paths but not the effective resistance between power/ground bumps and ESD devices. Because of the presence of multiple alternate paths between bumps and ESD cells, the resistance of least resistive path will be considerably higher than the effective resistance.


The ESD Analysis (report_esd_network) flow in Voltus allows you to perform different types of effective resistance checks, such as bump to clamp, bump to bump, and clamp to clamp, and current density analysis during an ESD zap event. This command checks whether every power or ground bump has an ESD device, and the device is placed in such a way that it does not violate the effective resistance or current density limit. The objective of ESD Checker is to easily identify the violations caused by high effective resistance or the current density above the specified threshold value. It also performs isolation checks, that is, if any bump does not have a path to an ESD cell (indicating existence of isolated bumps), or if a clamp is not connected to any bump (indicating existence of isolated clamps).

In addition, you can perform the Charged Device Model (CDM) based ESD analysis (report_esd_voltage) that allows you to report violations where potential difference across driver/receiver is greater than the given threshold.
